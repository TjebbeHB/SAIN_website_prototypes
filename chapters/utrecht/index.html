Two free courses run in Utrecht

Applications are closed. Sign ups for the next cohort will open next semester. Join the community (opens in a new tab) and you will hear when they open.

AI Safety Fundamentals has run three times in Utrecht, reaching more than 100 participants: BSc and MSc students, researchers, engineers, and public-sector staff. Four weeks, one hour of reading and a one-hour lecture a week, at Utrecht University in the city centre. In week four you choose a technical path, a governance path, or both. You get a certificate for attending all sessions; no previous background is needed.

Sessions, autumn 2026

  1. 1IntroductionWed 23 Sep · 18:00-19:00 · Janskerkhof 2-3, Room 0.19High-level overview of AI safety, why it matters now, capabilities and diffusion, types of risks, and solution strategies, technical and governance.
  2. 2Types of risks and incidentsWed 30 Sep · 18:00-19:00 · Janskerkhof 2-3, Room 0.19Social harms, frontier risks, misuse, loss of control; real-world cases and how research lags behind deployment.
  3. 3Why AI safety is difficultWed 7 Oct · 18:00-19:00 · Janskerkhof 15A, Room 101Industry incentives, funding gaps, expert disagreement, slow regulation against fast labs, geopolitical race dynamics.
  4. 4Path specialisationThe fourth week splits in two. Take one path, or both.Technical AI safetyWed 14 Oct · 18:00-19:00 · Janskerkhof 2-3, Room 0.19Jailbreaking, the scalable oversight problem, RLHF and reward signals.GovernanceThu 15 Oct · 18:00-19:00 · Drift 23, Room 103Overview of the EU AI Act, accountability in AI systems, international actors in AI safety.

The technical course teaches from ARENA, an open technical AI safety curriculum; SAIN Utrecht is the first SAIN chapter to teach from it. Four weeks of weekly lectures (about 90 minutes) plus hands-on notebooks, in person at Utrecht University and streamed online. Prerequisites: Python, plus linear algebra and probability. You get the certificate by completing the notebooks and attending in person.

Weekly themes

  1. 1Transformers and mechanistic interpretability (opening a model up to study what its internals compute)
  2. 2Probing and representations
  3. 3PPO and RLHF (the techniques used to train models from human feedback)
  4. 4RLHF, GRPO and reward hacking

These courses are independently run by SAIN Utrecht and are not affiliated with Utrecht University. Questions: eduutr@safeainetherlands.org.

Between cohorts, two discussion groups keep meeting: one on technical AI safety, currently a mechanistic interpretability reading group, and one on AI governance and policy.
